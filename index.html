Teil 1 — Leseverstehen & Schreiben

Summary
Aufgabe 1 · Zusammenfassung eines englischen Texts
Aufgabe 1
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Den Text sinngemäß in eigenen Worten zusammenfassen
  • Keine eigene Meinung -- rein sachlich bleiben
  • Hauptaussagen rausfiltern, Details weglassen
  • Reihenfolge des Originals nicht zwingend folgen
  • Nur Infos aus dem Text -- kein Vorwissen einbringen

Häufige Fehler

  • Zu lang -- eine Summary ist kurz und kompakt
  • Direkte Zitate aus dem Text verwenden
  • Eigene Meinung oder Bewertung einfließen lassen
  • Zu viele Details, Beispiele aus dem Original übernehmen
  • Mit "In this text..." anfangen (zu generisch)
1
Topic sentence -- Worum geht es? Autor, Quelle, Hauptthema in 1 Satz
2
Main points -- Die wichtigsten Aussagen des Texts in logischer Reihenfolge
3
Conclusion -- Fazit oder Schlussaussage des Texts (falls vorhanden)

Nützliche Phrasen

The article/text deals with... The author argues that... According to the text... The main point is that... Furthermore, the text states... In conclusion, the author... The text focuses on... As a result,...
✦ BEISPIEL — Summary zum Thema "Social Media & Mental Health"The article "Scrolling into Darkness" by journalist Amy Chen, published in The Guardian in 2023, examines the growing impact of social media on the mental health of teenagers. → Topic sentence Chen argues that platforms such as Instagram and TikTok are deliberately designed to maximise screen time, which leads to increased anxiety, poor sleep, and a distorted self-image among young users. She further points out that algorithmic content feeds often amplify negative emotions rather than positive ones. → Main points In conclusion, the author calls for stricter regulation of social media platforms and urges parents to monitor their children’s online activity more carefully. → Conclusion

Outline
Aufgabe 1 · Strukturierte Gliederung
Aufgabe 1
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Strukturierte Gliederung eines Arguments oder Texts
  • Stichpunkte, keine ganzen Sätze
  • Hierarchisch aufbauen (I. → A. → 1.)
  • Logische Argumentation zeigen
  • Nur die wichtigsten Punkte -- keine Details

Häufige Fehler

  • Ganze Sätze schreiben statt Stichpunkte
  • Zu viele Unterpunkte -- 2--3 pro Abschnitt reichen
  • Keine klare Hierarchie erkennbar
  • Eigene Meinung reinbringen (wenn nicht gefragt)
I
Introduction -- Topic + Thesis statement
II
Main Body -- A. First argument / B. Second argument / C. Counterargument
III
Conclusion -- Summary + final statement / recommendation
💡 Tipp: Bei einer Outline musst du nicht einen vollständigen Text schreiben. Stichpunkte wie "→ social media increases anxiety in teens" reichen völlig aus. Bewertung geht nach Struktur und Logik, nicht nach Sprache.
✦ BEISPIEL — Outline: "Should social media be banned for under-16s?"I. Introduction A. Hook: 1 in 3 teens report anxiety linked to social media B. Thesis: Social media should be strictly regulated for minors II. Main Body A. Argument 1: Negative impact on mental health 1. Comparison culture → low self-esteem 2. Sleep disruption due to screen time B. Argument 2: Exposure to harmful content 1. Algorithms promote extreme content 2. Cyberbullying increasingly common C. Counterargument: Social media enables connection 1. True, but offline alternatives exist 2. Benefits do not outweigh risks III. Conclusion A. Summary of main points B. Call for age verification laws C. Final statement: Protect young people now

Text Analysis
Aufgabe 2 · Analyse mit Stilmitteln
Aufgabe 2
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Text auf Inhalt + sprachliche Mittel analysieren
  • Stilmittel nennen, belegen (Zitat), Wirkung erklären
  • Immer: Beleg → Name → Wirkung (P-E-E Methode)
  • Auf Struktur, Ton, Perspektive eingehen
  • Fiktional: Erzählperspektive, Charaktere, Setting

Wichtige Stilmittel

  • Metaphor -- indirekter Vergleich
  • Simile -- "like" / "as" Vergleich
  • Alliteration -- gleiche Anfangsbuchstaben
  • Repetition -- Wiederholung zur Betonung
  • Rhetorical question -- Frage ohne Antwort
  • Irony/Sarcasm -- Gegenteil gemeint
  • Hyperbole -- Übertreibung
  • Personification -- Vermenschlichung
1
Introduction -- Titel, Autor, Textsorte, Thema, Intention in 2--3 Sätzen
2
Content -- Kurze Zusammenfassung der Hauptaussage
3
Language Analysis -- Stilmittel mit Beleg + Wirkung (P-E-E)
4
Conclusion -- Gesamtwirkung + Intention des Autors

P-E-E Methode (Point -- Evidence -- Effect)

The author uses... to suggest/show/highlight... The use of [device] creates a feeling of... This implies that... The [metaphor] "..." conveys... By using..., the author emphasises... This has the effect of... The repetition of "..." reinforces... The rhetorical question makes the reader...
✦ BEISPIEL — Text Analysis (Auszug, Thema: Environment)The article "A Planet on Fire" by George Monbiot is a non-fictional opinion piece published in 2022, which aims to persuade readers to take urgent action against climate change. → Intro Monbiot employs the metaphor of the planet being “on fire” to convey the sense of immediate crisis and destruction. This creates a vivid image of danger and urgency in the reader’s mind, making the threat feel tangible rather than abstract. → P-E-E Furthermore, the use of rhetorical questions such as “How many more warnings do we need?” directly implicates the reader, creating a sense of personal responsibility and guilt. This technique prevents the audience from remaining passive. → P-E-E Overall, Monbiot’s piece effectively combines emotive language and rhetorical devices to shock the reader into action. His intention is not merely to inform, but to provoke an immediate emotional and political response. → Conclusion

Cartoon / Image Analysis
Aufgabe 2 · Diskontinuierliche Texte
Aufgabe 2
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Bild beschreiben (was ist zu sehen?)
  • Botschaft / Message interpretieren
  • Symbolik, Ironie, Übertreibung erkennen
  • Kontext / aktueller Bezug herstellen
  • Stilmittel der Bildsprache benennen

Bildsprachliche Mittel

  • Symbolism -- Objekte stehen für Ideen
  • Exaggeration/Caricature -- übertriebene Darstellung
  • Irony -- Gegensatz Bild/Text
  • Labeling -- Beschriftungen im Bild
  • Juxtaposition -- Gegensätze nebeneinander
  • Size/Proportion -- Größenverhältnisse
1
Description -- Was ist zu sehen? (foreground, background, characters)
2
Interpretation -- Was bedeutet es? Symbolik? Botschaft?
3
Devices -- Welche Mittel werden eingesetzt + Wirkung?
4
Context -- Bezug zum Thema / zur Realität herstellen
The cartoon shows/depicts... In the foreground/background... The cartoonist uses... to symbolise... This is meant to criticise/highlight... The exaggeration of... suggests... The overall message is...
✦ BEISPIEL — Cartoon Analysis (Thema: Media & Truth)The cartoon depicts a large television set in the foreground displaying a smiling news anchor, while in the background a city is shown in flames. A family sits comfortably on a sofa, apparently unaware of the disaster behind them. → Description The central message of the cartoon is that mainstream media distorts reality and prevents ordinary people from recognising the true state of the world. The juxtaposition of the calm, cheerful TV screen against the burning city symbolises the gap between media representation and reality. → Interpretation The cartoonist uses exaggeration — the enormous flames contrast sharply with the family’s relaxed posture — to highlight how desensitised society has become to crisis. The labelling of the TV as “Breaking News” adds irony, as the truly “breaking” events go unnoticed. → Devices In the context of today’s media landscape, this cartoon serves as a sharp critique of sensationalist journalism and media bias. → Context

Essay / Comment
Aufgabe 3 · Argumentativer Text
Aufgabe 3
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Klare These aufstellen und verteidigen
  • Pro- und Contra-Argumente abwägen
  • Formelle Sprache -- keine Umgangssprache
  • Eigene Position klar am Ende vertreten
  • Logische Übergänge zwischen Absätzen

Häufige Fehler

  • Keine klare Position beziehen
  • Argumente ohne Belege / Beispiele
  • Zu kurze Absätze (mind. 3--4 Sätze pro Punkt)
  • Informelle Sprache ("I think it's pretty bad")
  • Keine Gegenargumente berücksichtigen
1
Introduction -- Hook + Kontext + These (eigene Position)
2
Argument 1 -- Hauptargument + Beleg + Erklärung
3
Argument 2 -- Weiteres Argument + Beispiel
4
Counterargument -- Gegenargument nennen + entkräften
5
Conclusion -- These wiederholen, Fazit, Ausblick
It is widely argued that... One could argue that... However, it must be considered... Furthermore / Moreover / In addition... Despite this, ... In conclusion, it is clear that... Taking everything into account... While X, it is important to note...
✦ BEISPIEL — Essay (Thema: "Should renewable energy replace fossil fuels entirely?")As the effects of climate change become increasingly visible, the question of whether renewable energy should fully replace fossil fuels has moved to the centre of public debate. This essay will argue that, despite practical challenges, a complete transition to renewables is both necessary and achievable. → Introduction + Thesis First and foremost, the continued use of fossil fuels poses an existential threat to our planet. Scientific evidence overwhelmingly shows that carbon emissions from coal, oil and gas are the primary driver of global warming. By investing in solar, wind and hydroelectric power, nations can drastically reduce their carbon footprint and prevent the worst outcomes of climate change. → Argument 1 Furthermore, renewable energy offers significant economic advantages in the long term. While the initial infrastructure costs are high, the operational costs of solar and wind energy are far lower than those of fossil fuel plants. Countries such as Germany and Denmark have already demonstrated that a renewable-based economy can be both stable and competitive. → Argument 2 Admittedly, critics argue that renewables are unreliable due to their dependence on weather conditions. However, rapid advances in battery storage technology are making this concern increasingly irrelevant. With sufficient investment, a stable and fully renewable grid is well within reach. → Counterargument In conclusion, the transition to renewable energy is not merely desirable — it is essential. Governments must act decisively now to secure a sustainable future for the next generation. → Conclusion

Speech / Rede
Aufgabe 3 · Persuasive Rede
Aufgabe 3
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Direkte Ansprache des Publikums
  • Rhetorische Mittel bewusst einsetzen
  • Klar überzeugend / persuasiv argumentieren
  • Kontext beachten: Wer spricht? Wo? Zu wem?
  • Starke Eröffnung und starkes Ende

Rhetorische Mittel für Reden

  • Rhetorical questions -- Publikum einbeziehen
  • Repetition / Anaphora -- Wirkung verstärken
  • Rule of three -- drei Punkte nacheinander
  • Direct address -- "You and I know that..."
  • Call to action -- Zum Handeln auffordern
  • Inclusive language -- "we", "our", "together"
1
Opening -- Anrede + starker Einstieg (Frage, Zitat, Statistik)
2
Main body -- 2--3 Argumente, klar gegliedert, mit Beispielen
3
Counterargument -- kurz ansprechen + widerlegen
4
Closing -- Call to action + unvergesslicher letzter Satz
Ladies and gentlemen,... Dear fellow students,... Ask yourself: ... We cannot afford to ignore... The time for action is now. Together, we can... I urge you to... Let me leave you with this thought: Thank you for your attention.
✦ BEISPIEL — Speech (Thema: "We must act on climate change now")Dear fellow students, teachers, and guests — thank you for being here today. → Opening / Anrede Let me ask you something. When was the last time you stepped outside and breathed air that did not carry the weight of a crisis? Because whether we acknowledge it or not, our planet is in danger — and we are the generation that must respond. → Hook / Rhetorical question The facts are undeniable. Global temperatures have risen by over 1.1 degrees Celsius since the industrial revolution. Wildfires, floods, and droughts are no longer rare events — they are the new normal. And yet, our governments continue to prioritise short-term profit over long-term survival. → Argument with evidence Some may say that individual action is pointless — that one person cannot change a global system. But history tells a different story. Every great movement began with individuals who refused to stay silent. We owe it to ourselves, to our communities, and to the generations that will follow us to stand up and demand change. → Counterargument So I urge you — write to your representatives. Make sustainable choices. Speak up. Because the time for waiting is over. The time for action is now. → Call to action Thank you. → Closing

Letter to the Editor
Aufgabe 3 · Formeller Leserbrief
Aufgabe 3
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Reaktion auf einen Artikel / Bericht
  • Formelle Sprache und formeller Briefaufbau
  • Bezug auf den Artikel herstellen
  • Eigene Position klar argumentieren
  • Höflicher aber bestimmter Ton

Formaler Aufbau

  • Kein Absenderadresse nötig im Abi
  • Anrede: Dear Editor,
  • Erster Satz: Bezug auf Artikel
  • Abschluss: Yours faithfully/sincerely,
  • Name am Ende nicht vergessen
1
Opening -- "I am writing in response to your article about..."
2
Position -- Eigene Meinung/These klar formulieren
3
Arguments -- 2--3 Argumente mit Belegen
4
Closing -- Forderung oder Appell + formeller Abschluss
I am writing in response to... I was [pleased/concerned] to read... I strongly believe that... It is deeply concerning that... I urge your readers to consider... Yours faithfully, / Yours sincerely,
✦ BEISPIEL — Letter to the Editor (Thema: Screen time in schools)Dear Editor, → Salutation I am writing in response to your article “Tablets in Every Classroom”, published on 14th March. While I appreciate the optimism behind this initiative, I am deeply concerned about the implications of increasing screen time for young learners. → Opening / Bezug auf Artikel Research consistently shows that excessive screen use negatively affects children’s attention spans and sleep quality. Replacing textbooks with tablets may therefore hinder rather than enhance learning, particularly for primary school students whose cognitive development depends on varied, tactile experiences. → Argument 1 Moreover, the digital divide remains a serious issue. Not all families can afford reliable internet access at home, meaning homework assigned via online platforms will disadvantage the most vulnerable students. → Argument 2 I urge your readers and local policymakers to consider a more balanced approach — one that integrates technology thoughtfully rather than wholesale. Our children deserve an education that prepares them for the real world, not one that chains them to a screen. → Appeal / Closing argument Yours sincerely, A concerned parent → Formal closing

Blog Post
Aufgabe 3 · Semi-formeller Meinungstext
Aufgabe 3
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Lockerer, aber nicht zu informeller Ton
  • Direkte Leseransprache erlaubt
  • Persönliche Meinung und Erfahrungen einbringen
  • Titel / Überschrift nicht vergessen!
  • Einladend und interessant schreiben

Ton & Stil

  • Contractions erlaubt: it's, don't, I've
  • Leser direkt ansprechen: "you", "we"
  • Rhetorische Fragen einbauen
  • Kurze, lebendige Sätze möglich
  • Humor / persönliche Anekdoten möglich
1
Headline -- Catchy Überschrift (Frage, Provokation, Wortspiel)
2
Hook -- Einstieg der Leser fesselt (Frage, Statistik, Story)
3
Main body -- Argumente / Punkte mit persönlichem Bezug
4
Closing -- Fazit + Aufruf an Leser (Kommentar, Diskussion)
Have you ever wondered...? Let me tell you something... Personally, I believe... What do you think? Let me know... Here's the thing:... If you ask me,... The bottom line is...
✦ BEISPIEL — Blog Post (Thema: Fast Fashion)Is Your Wardrobe Destroying the Planet? → Catchy Headline Okay, I know that sounds dramatic. But hear me out — because the last time I ordered three t-shirts for €15 online, I didn’t stop to think about what that actually costs. Not just in euros. → Hook / Personal anecdote Fast fashion is one of the most polluting industries on the planet. The fashion industry produces around 10% of global carbon emissions — more than aviation and shipping combined. And most of those cute tops we buy end up in landfill within a year. Sounds familiar, right? → Main point + facts Personally, I’ve started shopping second-hand, and honestly? It’s way more fun than scrolling through the same five styles on every high-street website. You find unique pieces, save money, and — bonus — you’re not funding a system built on exploitation. → Personal opinion / tip The bottom line is this: every purchase is a vote. And I, for one, am done voting for a system that’s burning the planet for profit. What about you? → Closing / Reader engagement

Open Letter
Aufgabe 3 · Offener Brief
Aufgabe 3
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Formeller Brief an eine öffentliche Person/Institution
  • Für ein breiteres Publikum geschrieben
  • Klare Forderung oder Botschaft
  • Ton: respektvoll aber direkt und überzeugend
  • Briefformat einhalten

Unterschied zum Letter to Editor

  • Adressiert an Person/Organisation, nicht Editor
  • Wird oft veröffentlicht -- für alle lesbar
  • Tone etwas persönlicher möglich
  • Klare Forderung/Appell am Ende
1
Salutation -- "Dear Mr President / Dear Mayor of Berlin,..."
2
Introduction -- Wer schreibt? Warum? Worum geht es?
3
Arguments -- 2--3 klare Punkte mit Belegen
4
Demand/Appeal -- Konkrete Forderung oder Bitte
5
Closing -- "Yours sincerely," + Name
I am writing on behalf of... As a concerned citizen/student... It is time to take action on... We call upon you to... The evidence clearly shows... Yours sincerely,
✦ BEISPIEL — Open Letter (Thema: An den Bürgermeister von Berlin)Dear Mayor of Berlin, → Salutation We, the students of Berlin’s secondary schools, are writing to draw your attention to an issue that affects thousands of young people in our city every single day: the lack of affordable and safe cycling infrastructure. → Introduction / Wer schreibt? Warum? Berlin has committed to becoming a green city by 2030. Yet many of us risk our lives cycling to school on roads without protected bike lanes. Last year alone, over 600 cyclists were injured in traffic accidents in our city. This is unacceptable. → Argument 1 mit Fakt Furthermore, a safer cycling network would benefit not only students but the entire city — reducing traffic, improving air quality, and encouraging a healthier lifestyle for all residents. The investment is modest; the returns are enormous. → Argument 2 We therefore call upon you to prioritise the expansion of protected cycling lanes in all districts of Berlin, beginning with the routes most commonly used by schoolchildren. → Concrete demand Yours sincerely, The Students of Berlin → Formal closing

Sprachmittlung -- Grundprinzip
Teil 2 · Deutsch → Englisch sinngemäß übertragen
Teil 2
▼

Das Wichtigste

  • Kein wörtliches Übersetzen! -- sinngemäß übertragen
  • Nur die für die Aufgabe relevanten Infos rausnehmen
  • Adressatengerecht schreiben -- Ton anpassen
  • Eigene Einleitung/Überleitung schreiben
  • Deutschen Text im Kopf lassen -- auf Englisch neu formulieren

Häufige Fehler

  • Wort-für-Wort Übersetzung → klingt unnatürlich
  • Zu viele/zu wenige Infos aus dem Text
  • Falscher Ton (zu formell/informell)
  • Keine Einleitung zum Kontext
  • Grammatik-Fehler durch deutschen Satzbau
⚠️ Achtung: Bei der Sprachmittlung wirst du bewertet auf: Inhaltsvollständigkeit, adressatengerechten Ton UND sprachliche Korrektheit. Alle drei Bereiche zählen!
I came across an article about... I thought you might be interested in... According to a German article,... The text mentions that... In other words,... The author points out that...
✦ BEISPIEL — Sprachmittlung Grundprinzip (Deutscher Ausgangstext → Englisch)Deutscher Text (Aufgabe): „Laut einer neuen Studie verbringen Jugendliche in Deutschland durchschnittlich 6 Stunden täglich vor Bildschirmen. Experten warnen vor ernsthaften Folgen für die Gesundheit, darunter Schlafstörungen und Konzentrationsprobleme. Schulen fordern deshalb ein Umdenken im Umgang mit digitalen Medien." According to a recent German study, teenagers spend an average of six hours per day in front of screens. → Keine wörtl. Übersetzung Health experts have raised serious concerns about the consequences of this trend, pointing to sleep disorders and difficulties concentrating as key issues. → sinngemäß übertragen As a result, schools across Germany are now calling for a fundamental rethink in the way digital media is used — both in the classroom and at home. → relevante Info vollständig

Mediation -- E-Mail
Teil 2 · Häufigste Mediation-Textsorte
Teil 2
▼

Formales nicht vergessen

  • Subject: Betreff oben drüber
  • Anrede: Dear Mr/Ms... (formell) oder Hi [Name],
  • Abschluss: Best regards / Kind regards / Cheers
  • Deinen Namen am Ende
  • Ton nach Empfänger: Chef → formell, Freund → informell

Inhalt

  • Kurze Einleitung: Warum schreibst du?
  • Wichtigste Infos aus dem deutschen Text übertragen
  • Eigene Kommentare / Meinung nur wenn gefragt
  • Kurz und präzise -- keine Roman-Länge
1
Subject: Kurzer, aussagekräftiger Betreff
2
Salutation -- Dear.../Hi...
3
Opening line -- Kontext erklären: "I came across..."
4
Main content -- Relevante Infos aus deutschem Text auf Englisch
5
Closing -- "Let me know if..." + Best regards + Name
Subject: Info about [topic] I hope this email finds you well. I recently read an article about... and thought you'd find it interesting. According to the article,... The key points are:... Let me know if you have any questions. Best regards, / Kind regards,
✦ BEISPIEL — Mediation E-Mail (an englischsprachigen Austauschpartner)Subject: Interesting article about screen time in Germany → Subject Hi Tom, → Informelle Anrede (Freund) I hope you’re doing well! I recently came across a German article about teenagers and screen time, and I thought you might find it interesting given our discussion last week. → Kontext / Einleitung According to the article, young people in Germany spend an average of six hours a day looking at screens. Doctors and health experts are warning that this leads to serious problems, including poor sleep and difficulty focusing in school. Because of this, many schools are now pushing for new rules about how students use digital devices. → Inhalt sinngemäß auf Englisch Personally, I think it’s a topic worth discussing — what’s the situation like in your country? Let me know what you think! → Eigene Meinung (wenn gefragt) Best regards, Dean → Informal closing + Name

Mediation -- Summary / Brief
Teil 2 · Für englischsprachige Person zusammenfassen
Teil 2
▼

Was zu tun ist

  • Deutschen Text für englischsprachige Person aufbereiten
  • Kontext erklären (woher kommt der Text?)
  • Ton je nach Aufgabenstellung anpassen
  • Nur relevante Infos für den spezifischen Empfänger

Tipps

  • Immer fragen: Was braucht diese Person zu wissen?
  • Kulturelle Begriffe erklären (z.B. "Gymnasium" = grammar school)
  • Zahlen und Fakten präzise übertragen
  • Eigene Meinung nur wenn ausdrücklich gefragt
I would like to summarise a German article for you. The article was published in... To give you some context,... In Germany, this means that... The most important points are... I hope this gives you a good overview.
💡 Tipp: Bei kulturspezifischen deutschen Begriffen (Abitur, Gymnasium, Bundesrat etc.) kurz auf Englisch erklären -- der englische Empfänger kennt das nicht!
✦ BEISPIEL — Mediation Summary (für englischsprachige Kollegin)I would like to summarise a German newspaper article for you that may be relevant to our upcoming project. → Einleitung / Kontext The article, published in the Süddeutsche Zeitung, discusses a new German government initiative to reduce plastic waste in supermarkets. To give you some context: in Germany, supermarkets are now legally required to offer reusable alternatives to single-use plastic packaging by 2025. → Kultureller Kontext erklärt The most important points are as follows: firstly, the new law affects all retailers with more than five employees. Secondly, consumers will be able to bring their own containers for products such as meat, cheese and salads. Finally, experts predict that this measure could eliminate up to 40,000 tonnes of plastic waste per year. → Schlüsselinfos präzise I hope this gives you a useful overview. Please let me know if you need any further details. → Abschluss
